# Sector-Specific Metrics for US Stocks

Always layer sector-appropriate metrics on top of the general QARP framework. Generic P/E comparisons across sectors are meaningless — a bank at 12x and a SaaS at 12x P/S are both "normal".

---

## Software / SaaS

Standard margins/P/E mislead here because growth is expensed (S&M) and SBC is huge.

| Metric | What it measures | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Rule of 40** | Revenue growth % + FCF margin % | > 40 healthy; > 60 elite |
| **Gross margin** | Software economics check | > 70%; < 60% means it's not really a software business |
| **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** | Expansion within existing customers | > 110% good; > 120% elite |
| **SBC / Revenue** | Real cost of "profitability" | < 10% mature; 15-25% common but dilutive |
| **EV / Revenue (fwd)** | Valuation standard for SaaS | 5-10x normal; > 15x needs hypergrowth + Rule of 60 |
| **FCF margin** | True profitability | > 20% for mature SaaS |
| **Billings / RPO growth** | Leading demand indicator | Should track or lead revenue growth |

## Semiconductors

Deeply cyclical — never value on peak earnings.

| Metric | What it measures | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Gross margin trend** | Pricing power through cycle | > 50% fabless/design; 40s% for fabs |
| **Inventory days** | Cycle position warning | Spiking inventory = downturn ahead |
| **Capex intensity** | Fabs (TSM, Intel) vs fabless (NVDA, AMD) | Fabless = better FCF; fabs need utilization > 80% |
| **Through-cycle P/E** | Valuation | Cheap P/E on peak earnings is a trap; look at mid-cycle EPS |
| **Design wins / backlog** | Forward revenue visibility | Especially for auto/industrial semis |

## Banks & Financials

Don't use EV/EBITDA or Debt/Equity — meaningless for banks. Use:

| Metric | What it measures | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **ROE / ROTCE** | Core bank profitability | ROE > 12%, ROTCE > 15% good |
| **NIM** (Net Interest Margin) | Lending spread | 3%+ healthy for US banks |
| **Efficiency ratio** | Cost / revenue | < 60% good; < 50% excellent |
| **CET1 ratio** | Regulatory capital buffer | > 10-11% comfortable |
| **P/TBV** | Valuation standard | < 1x cheap (ask why), 1.5-2x for quality (JPM) |
| **Net charge-offs / provisions** | Credit quality | Spikes = credit cycle turning |

## Healthcare / Pharma / Biotech

| Metric | What it measures | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Patent cliff exposure** | Revenue at risk | Check % of revenue from drugs losing exclusivity in 5yr |
| **Pipeline (Phase 2/3 assets)** | Future growth | Search "[company] pipeline" — approvals replace cliff losses? |
| **R&D / Revenue** | Innovation investment | 15-25% for pharma |
| **For pre-revenue biotech** | Cash runway vs trial timeline | Runway must cover next readout + 12mo; binary risk — venture sizing only |
| **For insurers (UNH etc.)** | Medical loss ratio (MLR) | Lower = better margins; ~80-85% typical |

## Retail / Consumer

| Metric | What it measures | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Same-store sales (comps)** | Organic growth ex-new stores | Positive comps = healthy; negative 2+ yrs = decline |
| **Inventory turns** | Operational efficiency | Higher better; falling turns + rising markdowns = trouble |
| **Operating margin** | Sector-realistic | 3-8% grocery/mass; 10%+ specialty; COST runs ~3.5% by design (membership model) |
| **E-commerce mix & growth** | Channel resilience | Growing share without margin collapse |
| **Membership/loyalty economics** | Recurring revenue (COST, AMZN) | Renewal rates > 90% = moat |

## REITs

Normal EPS is meaningless (depreciation distorts it). Use:

| Metric | What it measures | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **FFO / AFFO per share** | Real cash earnings | Use P/FFO instead of P/E (12-20x typical) |
| **Dividend / AFFO payout** | Sustainability | < 80% safe |
| **Occupancy & releasing spreads** | Demand health | Rising spreads = pricing power |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | Leverage (REITs run higher) | 5-6x normal; > 7x risky |
| **Sector matters most** | Data centers/towers vs offices | Secular winners vs secular decliners |

## Energy / Commodities

| Metric | What it measures | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **FCF breakeven price** | Resilience | Profitable at $45-50 WTI = quality shale |
| **Reserve life & replacement** | Sustainability of production | > 10 years proved reserves |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | Cycle survival | < 1x is the post-2020 discipline standard |
| **Capital returns framework** | Shareholder policy | Fixed + variable dividend + buybacks (e.g., majors) |
| **Never extrapolate peak prices** | Valuation discipline | Value on mid-cycle commodity prices |

## Mega-cap platforms (AAPL/MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN/META)

| Metric | What it measures | Notes |
|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **Segment growth** | The parts matter more than the whole | e.g., cloud growth %, services mix, ad revenue growth |
| **Capex cycle (AI era)** | Current investment supercycle | Watch capex/revenue and the FCF impact; ask what the return on AI capex is |
| **Buyback yield** | Capital return | Buybacks + dividends / market cap = total shareholder yield |
| **Regulatory/antitrust risk** | Ongoing for all of them | Headline risk ≠ thesis breaker, but monitor remedies |
